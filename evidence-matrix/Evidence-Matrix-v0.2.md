## Purpose

This matrix synthesises the current evidence corpus.

Statuses describe what the corpus currently supports within the stated scope. They are not universal policy recommendations.

Version v0.2 incorporates two causal-feasibility branches from Swedish municipal rescue services:

- **R9 — Uppsala 2012 K→A causal branch**
- **R10 — Historical 2002 first-full-A cohort**

These materially advance former Evidence Gap #3:

> merger / full-authority pooling vs cooperation vs independent provision on the same function.

The gap is now **partially closed for Swedish rescue services**, but not resolved generally.

---

## Status vocabulary

- **Supported** — current evidence broadly supports the proposition within its stated scope.
- **Conditionally supported** — evidence supports it under identifiable conditions.
- **Mixed** — credible evidence points in materially different directions.
- **Unresolved** — insufficient evidence.
- **Weak** — some relevant evidence exists but is limited or poorly matched.
- **Contradicted** — current evidence conflicts with the proposition.
- **Exploratory** — proposition is sufficiently motivated to test but evidence remains immature.

# Synthesis claims

## C1 — Governance fit

> **Current evidence is more consistent with conditional governance fit than with a universal decentralisation, centralisation or integration principle.**

The rescue-service results strengthen this claim because two independently frozen causal branches within the **same function** do not yield a common treatment direction.

**Status:** Conditionally supported  
**Confidence:** **Medium–High**  
**Change from v0.1:** confidence increased from Medium.

---

## C2 — Formal decentralisation is an insufficient proxy for subsidiarity

> **Formal transfer of responsibility does not reliably establish substantive decision proximity.**

Unchanged.

**Status:** Conditionally supported  
**Confidence:** Medium

---

## C3 — Functional differentiation matters

> **Appropriate governance architecture varies substantially by function.**

The rescue programme adds an important qualification:

> architecture may also vary **within a function** according to baseline coordination, history and transition conditions.

**Status:** Conditionally supported  
**Confidence:** Medium–High

---

## C4 — Scale can be factorized

> **Some benefits associated with larger scale can be obtained through shared functions, coordination or financing without pooling all underlying authority.**

R9 makes this more concrete: Jönköping/RäddSam F, Piteå–Älvsbyn/Femkanten, Halmstad/RäddSam Halland and Skåne Nordväst represent substantive K architectures rather than institutional “non-treatment”.

R9 does not show a large clear incremental gain from adding A to mature K. R10 shows that first full-A pooling can coincide with adverse short-run outcomes.

**Status:** Conditionally supported  
**Confidence:** Medium

The confidence does not rise above Medium because neither causal branch directly decomposes the benefits generated by each K component.

---

## C4a — Authority pooling is not a monotonic scale upgrade

> **Full pooled authority should not be treated as a mechanically superior point on a one-dimensional scale from local to central governance. Its marginal value appears conditional on what coordination, capacity and institutional relationships already exist.**

Basis:

- R9 — Uppsala K→A
- R10 — Historical 2002 first-A cohort
- H8
- H21
- S5

R9 gives a modest favorable K→A signal.

R10 gives a large adverse first-A signal on a different but related fire outcome.

The evidence therefore rejects a simple empirical assumption that “more pooled authority” has a uniformly beneficial outcome direction.

It does **not** establish that less pooled authority is uniformly better.

**Status:** Conditionally supported / heterogeneous evidence  
**Confidence:** **Medium**

---

## C5 — Coordination is a design variable, not merely a cost

> **When authority and capacity are distributed across multiple nodes, coordination architecture is itself a major governance variable rather than merely friction to be eliminated by consolidation.**

The rescue-service programme strengthens the empirical relevance of distinguishing:

- operational coordination `K`;
- formal pooled authority `A`;
- and the transition between them.

**Status:** Conditionally supported  
**Confidence:** Medium–High

---

## C6 — Human capability is an emerging missing variable

Unchanged.

**Status:** Exploratory  
**Confidence:** Low

# Evidence Gap #3 — updated status

### v0.1

> Comparison of merger vs cooperation vs independent provision.

**Status:** unresolved.

### v0.2

**Status: PARTIALLY CLOSED FOR SWEDISH RESCUE SERVICES**

What is now observed:

1. A direct **K→A** case with multiple K-system counterfactuals: Uppsala 2012.
2. An independent cohort of **first verified full-A transitions**: Historical 2002.
3. Prespecified outcomes and pre-outcome design locks in both branches.
4. Materially different treatment directions across the two branches.

What remains unresolved:

- clean isolation of genuinely independent provision from weaker forms of K;
- multiple K→A treated systems rather than one Uppsala treatment unit;
- common outcome definitions across historical and B20 regimes;
- causal identification of which K components substitute for A;
- transition costs versus persistent steady-state effects;
- heterogeneity by geography, scale, staffing and prior collaboration;
- whether authority form affects response time, prevention, dispatch, suppression or reporting through different mechanisms.

The old gap should therefore no longer be described as simply “no direct comparison”.

The remaining question is more precise:

> **Under what baseline coordination and problem conditions does additional pooled authority improve outcomes, add little, or create transition/centralisation costs?**

---

# What v0.2 does NOT support

The current corpus does not justify:

- decentralisation is generally superior;
- centralisation is generally superior;
- full authority pooling is generally superior to cooperation;
- cooperation is generally superior to full authority pooling;
- common rescue boards generally improve outcomes;
- municipal rescue-service federations generally worsen outcomes;
- the Historical 2002 result is a generic “merger penalty”;
- the Uppsala result proves a generic “integration benefit”;
- the two rescue studies estimate the same treatment or the same outcome;
- the adverse 2002 arrival-spread signal proves poorer suppression after arrival.

---

# Updated priority evidence gaps

1. Direct measures of actual decision proximity.
2. Authority/information/resource mapping below formal administrative levels.
3. **Conditional A-vs-K-vs-independent comparison:** estimate the marginal effect of authority pooling conditional on pre-existing coordination intensity.
4. Mechanism decomposition in rescue services: response/coverage, dispatch, prevention, suppression and reporting.
5. Longitudinal evidence separating transition costs from steady-state effects.
6. Fiscal equalisation effects on service outcomes and incentives.
7. Swedish evidence on participation and capability development.
8. Operationalisation of systems reasoning.
9. Direct tests of architecture × capability interaction.
