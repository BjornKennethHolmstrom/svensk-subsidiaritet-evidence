# Räddningstjänst
## Cross-Branch Causal Synthesis v0.1

**Date:** 2026-09-30  
**Status:** SYNTHESIS OF TWO FROZEN EMPIRICAL BRANCHES  
**Question:** What do the Uppsala/B20 and Historical 2002 branches jointly say about full authority pooling versus cooperation or independent provision?

## 1. Why these branches should be compared — but not pooled

The research programme now contains two independently structured empirical branches concerning authority pooling in the same public function.

### Branch A — Uppsala 2012

Treatment margin: `K → A`.

Uppsala moved from long-standing operational cooperation to a common rescue board with Tierp and Östhammar.

Primary outcome: final building-fire extent beyond the start object per 100,000.

Primary strict result against Jönköping: **RRR = 0.931**, approximately 7% favorable.

Piteå–Älvsbyn gives **RRR = 0.935**. Three of four plausible system-level counterfactuals point favorable, while Halmstad points opposite. The result is statistically imprecise and favorable relative divergence is already visible in 2011, before the formal 2012 legal start.

### Branch B — Historical 2002 cohort

Treatment margin: `not yet full A → first verified full A` across five independent 2002 institutional events.

Primary outcome: building-fire spread beyond the start object **at rescue-service arrival** per 100,000.

Primary result: **IRR = 1.953**, approximately 95% adverse relative change.

The direction survives all frozen pre-window, item-nonresponse, structural-reporting-loss, leave-one-treated and leave-one-control analyses. All five treated events individually move in the adverse raw pre/post direction.

## 2. The results are genuinely different

They estimate different treatment margins. Uppsala asks about adding formal pooled authority to a mature operational-cooperation system. Historical 2002 asks about entry into first verified full pooled authority across several systems whose pre-A institutional histories vary.

They also measure different points in the fire process: Historical 2002 measures spread **at arrival**, whereas Uppsala/B20 measures **final extent**. They cover different institutional and measurement eras.

The divergence should therefore be treated as evidence about **heterogeneous institutional effects**, not as a failed replication.

## 3. What the two branches jointly weaken

The combined evidence weakens four simple models:

1. **“More pooled authority is always better.”** Not supported.
2. **“Operational cooperation is always better than pooled authority.”** Not supported.
3. **“Organisational form does not matter.”** Increasingly difficult to maintain; both branches generate non-trivial signals after prespecified institutional transitions.
4. **“Governance scale is one dimension.”** The evidence is more compatible with distinct dimensions such as formal authority `A` and operational coordination `K`.

## 4. Emerging hypothesis: marginal authority depends on baseline coordination

The most useful joint hypothesis is:

`Effect(A) = f(K0, history, transition, geography, capacity, ...)`

rather than `Effect(A) = constant`.

In words: **the effect of adding pooled authority may depend strongly on how much operational coordination already exists and on how the transition is implemented.**

This interpretation is not yet established. It is the most natural next hypothesis generated by the two branches.

## 5. Why the Historical 2002 outcome matters especially

The 2002 signal is strongest at spread beyond the start object **at arrival** and weakens at stricter severity thresholds. Total reported building fires are approximately neutral. This directs attention toward mechanisms operating before or around arrival: station/coverage geography, mobilisation, dispatch, travel, availability, transition of routines, incident composition, and reporting structure.

The present study cannot distinguish these explanations.

## 6. Why Uppsala may differ

Uppsala had long-standing rescue-service cooperation before formal A. The transition was closer to `high-K / lower-A → high-K / higher-A` than from isolated municipal provision to integration.

This creates a plausible path-dependence hypothesis: **formalisation after a mature cooperation network may behave differently from first full pooling where the operational system is itself being substantially reorganised.**

Again, this is a hypothesis generated by the evidence, not a conclusion already established by it.

## 7. Cooperation should be treated as a substantive governance state

Jönköping/RäddSam F, Piteå–Älvsbyn/Femkanten, Halmstad/RäddSam Halland and Skåne Nordväst are not appropriately described as simple “non-integrated controls”. They possess meaningful coordination architectures.

Future research should avoid the binary `local ↔ integrated` and instead map configurations such as `(A,K)` or more finely `(A,K_P,K_I,F,...)` where justified.

## 8. What about genuinely independent provision?

This remains the least resolved part of the original evidence gap. Historical controls are structurally “not yet full A”, but do not necessarily represent zero cooperation. Uppsala controls deliberately represent substantial K-without-A systems.

The programme can now say more about **A versus K-without-A** and heterogeneous first-A transitions than about **A versus genuinely independent low-K local provision**.

## 9. Current comparative assessment

| Question | Current evidence |
|---|---|
| Does full A universally improve outcomes? | No support for a universal claim. |
| Does full A universally worsen outcomes? | No support for a universal claim. |
| Can K exist as a substantial alternative to A? | Yes; well supported institutionally. |
| Does adding A to mature K produce a large improvement? | Not in Uppsala; point estimate is modest and uncertain. |
| Can first full-A transitions coincide with adverse short-run outcomes? | Yes; strongly signalled by Historical 2002. |
| Are A and K empirically interchangeable? | No. They should be modelled separately. |
| Is one architecture generally superior? | Unresolved and unlikely to have a context-free answer. |
| Is transition history likely to matter? | Increasingly plausible; direct causal test still needed. |
| Is independent provision cleanly compared with both K and A? | Not yet. |

## 10. Implication for the subsidiarity programme

The strongest emerging principle is not “decentralise”, “consolidate”, or “cooperate instead of consolidating”. It is:

> **Separate the governance functions being supplied by coordination and authority, then ask what marginal capability is gained or lost by moving each one.**

The empirical question becomes:

> **Given the coordination architecture already present, what additional problem is pooled authority solving, and what new costs or transition risks does it create?**

## 11. Current cross-branch finding

> **The two Swedish rescue-service causal branches do not support a universally signed effect of full authority pooling. Uppsala's transition from mature operational cooperation to pooled authority produces a modest favorable but imprecise signal, while the independent Historical 2002 cohort produces a large and internally robust adverse signal at the fire-spread-at-arrival threshold. Because the studies estimate different institutional margins, outcomes and periods, their divergence is best interpreted as evidence of heterogeneous and path-dependent effects rather than direct contradiction.**
>
> **Together they strengthen the proposition that operational coordination and formal authority are distinct governance variables. Cooperation can constitute a substantive high-capacity governance state without full authority pooling, while the marginal effect of adding or creating pooled authority appears conditional on prior coordination, implementation context and the outcome being measured.**
>
> **The evidence does not establish that cooperation, independent provision or pooled authority is generally superior. It does, however, substantially narrow the research question: the next causal target is the conditional marginal effect of A given baseline K, rather than another unconditional comparison of organisational labels.**

### Overall synthesis label

**EVIDENCE GAP #3: PARTIALLY CLOSED — DIRECTIONALLY MIXED, STRUCTURALLY INFORMATIVE**

- Confidence in universal A-vs-K ranking: **Low**
- Confidence that A and K must be modelled separately: **Medium–High**
- Confidence that treatment effects are context/path dependent: **Medium**
- Confidence in identified moderator mechanism: **Low**
