# Targeted Control Pool Reconstruction v0.1 — Uppsala 2012

**Status:** STRUCTURAL CONTROL POOL RECONSTRUCTED — PRE-MATCH OUTCOME SCREEN PENDING  
**Treatment:** Uppsala/Tierp/Östhammar common rescue board, effective 2012-01-01  
**Outcome firewall:** CLOSED for Uppsala treatment/post-treatment outcomes  
**Control-selection outcome window:** 2007–2009 only in the primary specification

## Executive result

The control problem changes materially once the K-layer is reconstructed.

Uppsala 2012 was **not** a transition from independent provision directly to full formal pooling. Uppsala's own 2011 decision material states that the 2009 investigation of deeper rescue-service cooperation built on county cooperation dating back to the **1990s**, including a **common leadership duty**. The same material records a formal study in 2009 and directional municipal decisions in autumn **2010** to work toward the common board that began on 1 January 2012.

The relevant treatment contrast is therefore better described as:

\[
	ext{existing operational cooperation (K)} ightarrow 	ext{formal pooled authority (A)}
\]

rather than:

\[
	ext{independent municipal provision} ightarrow A.
\]

This invalidates the previous idea that controls should preferentially be "K-clean". For Uppsala, a better control should instead resemble **K without A** and remain free of an A-commitment during the post window.

v0.1 identifies **4 independent control systems / 7 municipalities** worth pre-match screening.

## 1. Uppsala treatment-path reconstruction

### Pre-existing K

The 2011 Uppsala municipal decision memo says that the deeper-cooperation work initiated in 2009 built on rescue-service cooperation in Uppsala County that had started in the **1990s**, including a shared leadership duty.

This is a new K-history fact and should be added to the treatment-history corpus.

### Planning and commitment

- **2009:** formal study of deeper rescue-service coordination launched.
- **2010:** Tierp, Uppsala and Östhammar take directional decisions to work toward a common rescue board; Uppsala's municipal executive board decision is dated 6 October 2010.
- **2011:** final governance agreement/regulations prepared and adopted.
- **2012-01-01:** common rescue board begins, Uppsala as host municipality.

### Anticipation rule

The previous generic `t−1` anticipation assumption is too narrow for this event.

Primary specification:

- clean pre-match years: **2007–2009**
- commitment/implementation anticipation: **2010–2011**
- A-treatment: **2012+**

Conservative sensitivity:

- clean pre-match years: **2007–2008**
- planning/commitment anticipation: **2009–2011**
- A-treatment: **2012+**

2010 has already been observed as pre-A data in earlier feasibility work, but v0.1 no longer treats it as clean matching evidence.

## 2. Control eligibility rule

A full-window Uppsala control should satisfy:

1. **A = 0 through 2014**;
2. no identified commitment to enter A during 2012–2014;
3. meaningful operational cooperation **K** before Uppsala's A-treatment;
4. no known B20 measurement break unique to the candidate;
5. where multiple municipalities share one K-system, they count as **one independence cluster**, not independent controls;
6. outcome similarity is evaluated only after structural eligibility is fixed, using the locked **2007–2009** pre-match window.

This is a structural screen, not an outcome-optimized donor selection.

## 3. Reconstructed candidate systems

### A. RäddSam F / Jönköping — CORE

Jönköping is the strongest newly reconstructed candidate.

RäddSam F's document bank contains internal agreements dated **2007-11-27**, and Jönköping describes the system as boundaryless rescue-service cooperation with shared rescue-chief-on-duty capacity. At the same time, Jönköping's rescue service remains a municipal organization.

**v0.1 classification:** `CORE_A`.

Why it is attractive:

- K architecture is clearly established before Uppsala commitment;
- K includes leadership/resource sharing, not merely occasional mutual aid;
- no full-A transition or A-commitment through 2014 has been found;
- Jönköping is a substantially larger and more organizationally complex municipality than most existing late-treatment controls.

Residual task: one contemporary 2007–2014 municipal governance document should be archived before final A=0 lock.

### B. Skåne Nordväst / Höganäs–Båstad–Bjuv — one control cluster

Höganäs' handlingsprogram adopted in 2008 explicitly records shared **Räddningschef i beredskap**, a common staff commander, and regional operational cooperation in Skåne Nordväst.

Within this K-system:

- **Höganäs:** remained municipal own-regime and is the cleanest member-level candidate.
- **Bjuv:** K-exposed in Nordvästra Skåne; full A only from 2019.
- **Båstad:** remained outside full A until joining RSNV in 2024.

These three municipalities must **not** be counted as three independent controls. Their common K-system is one independence cluster.

**v0.1 classification:** Höganäs `CORE_A`; Bjuv/Båstad `SECONDARY_B`.

### C. RäddSam Halland / Halmstad — PROVISIONAL

A 2009 Halmstad source records that county rescue services already "work a lot together", while later material documents RäddSam Halland as a shared operational system. Halmstad itself is a municipal rescue-service organization.

**v0.1 classification:** `PROVISIONAL_B`.

The missing fact is the exact date/scope at which the shared leadership architecture became comparable to Uppsala's K-state. Halmstad should enter pre-match screening but should not be a final primary donor until that date is locked.

### D. Piteå–Älvsbyn / Femkanten — MECHANISM MATCH

Piteå and Älvsbyn provide the clearest documented **K-before-A trajectory** among the old late-treatment candidates.

- temporary shared rescue chief during 2010;
- formal three-year shared-chief/services agreement from 2011;
- boundaryless Femkanten cooperation plus common purchases, training and operational support;
- full common rescue board only from **2020**.

**v0.1 classification:** `MECHANISM_B`.

This is an excellent treatment-path analogue but a weaker scale match. Piteå and Älvsbyn count as one control system.

## 4. Explicit exclusions from the full-window control pool

### Helsingborg–Ängelholm–Örkelljunga / RSNV

These municipalities had strong K and therefore looked attractive under the new state-match rule. But later documentation records that their councils decided already in **2012** to form the federation starting 2015.

Thus 2013–2014 are not credibly untreated/no-anticipation years.

**Decision:** exclude as a full-window control. Retain as a K→A analogue.

### Klippan–Åstorp / Räddningstjänsten Söderåsen

They had a common rescue chief and shared incident commanders from **1996** and created a common rescue board in **2014**, with merger work during 2013.

**Decision:** exclude as a full-window control. Retain as a particularly informative K→A timing analogue.

### Härjedalen 2015

No comparable pre-2010 K architecture has yet been verified, and active integration into Jämtlands räddningstjänstförbund was already underway during 2014.

**Decision:** deprioritize for Uppsala control use.

### Ljusnarsberg 2018

No comparable pre-2010 shared-leadership K architecture has yet been verified; municipality is also very small.

**Decision:** deprioritize for the primary Uppsala donor pool.

## 5. Independence structure

The seven municipality candidates correspond to only **four control systems**:

1. `CTRL_RADDSAM_F_JKPG` — Jönköping;
2. `CTRL_SKANE_NW` — Höganäs, Båstad, Bjuv;
3. `CTRL_RADDSAM_HALLAND` — Halmstad;
4. `CTRL_PITEA_ALVSBYN` — Piteå, Älvsbyn.

Inference and donor weighting must respect this clustering. A model may use municipality-level rows, but it must not interpret correlated municipalities inside the same K-network as independent institutional replications.

## 6. Pre-match data lock

A 21-row request list has been generated for the seven municipality candidates:

- years **2007, 2008, 2009 only**;
- no 2010–2014 control outcomes are requested at this stage;
- this avoids choosing donors based on the period in which Uppsala is committed/treated.

The next data step is to retrieve:

- B20 locked severity counts for these 21 municipality-years;
- SCB mean-population denominators;
- pre-treatment structural covariates if available without outcome leakage.

Then donors can be screened on:

- pre-2009 level of the primary rate;
- 2007–2009 trajectory;
- population/urban-rural scale;
- station/resource architecture;
- risk-profile proxies.

Because only three outcome years remain in the primary pre-match window, outcome fit should **not** dominate structural similarity.

## 7. Design consequence

The prior public-B20 design was:

> Uppsala vs later exact-treatment events assumed to be clean controls.

v0.1 replaces that with:

> **Uppsala K→A vs a targeted donor pool of K-without-A systems, with explicit anticipation and network clustering.**

This is a materially better causal question.

However, it also makes the design more demanding. If the new donor pool fails pre-match plausibility, the correct conclusion is not to relax the K criterion; it is that the public-B20 Uppsala design may not support credible causal identification.
